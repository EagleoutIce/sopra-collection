\section{General}
\subsection{Why, wherefore, whence?}
    This \LaTeXe-package was written as part of the Sopra project in the
    winter semester of 2019 and summer semester of 2020, and serves as the
    basis for creating tables
    for \imptext{Team 20}. This documentation was created together with
    \T{sopra-base.cls} and the \T{sopra-documentation.sty} package.\par
    The \T{sopra-listings} package is used to visualize the
    individual code snippets.
    The corresponding package should also be embedded in this document: \scalebox{0.65}{\attachfile[subject={sopra-tables.sty}]{sopra-tables.sty}}.
\subsection{Dependencies}
    This package includes the following packages:
    \begin{multicols}{3}
        \begin{itemize}
            \def\pkgparse#1:#2\@nil{%

\subsection{Installation}
    The package is not distributed as a \T{.dtx} file, which is why the
    following options are available:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The package can be placed in the same directory as the document.
                In this case, the inclusion command reads:
\begin{plainlatex}
\usepackage{sopra-tables}
\end{plainlatex}
        \item The package can be placed in a subdirectory, or in a
                directory shipped alongside the document. In
                this case, it is specified via the (relative) path:
\begin{plainlatex}
\usepackage{./My/Path/to/sopra-tables}
\end{plainlatex}
        \item The package can be placed (via a symlink or similar)
              into your own \emph{texmf} tree.
              For example, on Linux using texlive,
              the package can be placed here: \bvoid{\~/texmf/tex/latex/}.
              The directory can be created and then
              updated using \bbash{texhash \~/texmf}. The
              package can now be used like any other installed package:
\begin{plainlatex}
\usepackage{sopra-tables}
\end{plainlatex}
    \end{itemize}

\subsection{Accepted Parameters}
    Like most packages, this package accepts arguments.
    For arguments with a \say{Counter} option, the one active by default is written first and the other in parentheses.
    So the following is called implicitly:
\begin{plainlatex}
    \usepackage[usetabu]{sopra-tables}
\end{plainlatex}
    While with:
\begin{plainlatex}
    \usepackage[notabu]{sopra-tables}
\end{plainlatex}
    we compile the document without the \T{tabu} package.

    \begin{argument}{usetabu}{notabu}
        Analogous to the \T{notabu} option of \T{sopra-requirements.sty}: by default, this package uses \T{tabu} for visual reasons to render the tables. Should the (bug) fixes contained in this package (no longer) work, or should the \T{booktabs} tables used in \T{notabu} be preferred, this option can be switched accordingly.  \notetext{Note: With \T{usetabu}, \cmd{relax} is set within \env{mtabular} so that no error/horizontal lines occur when one wants to write for both designs.}
    \end{argument}

    \begin{argument}{cpalette}{nocpalette}
        Offers support for \href{color-palettes}{https://github.com/EagleoutIce/color-palettes}. In this case, the table's highlighting is automatically adapted to the current palette.
    \end{argument}

    \begin{argument}{invert}{noinvert}
        Inverts the highlighting for the header row.
    \end{argument}

    \subsection{Colors}

    With \jmark[\T{usetabu}]{mrk:usetabu}, the color \T{MaterialHeaderColor} defines the color of the title column and \T{NextMaterialHeaderColor} the color in case of a page break. The colors can be changed like any other. Within \T{sopra-base}, a color adapted to the design is chosen automatically.

\section{Commands and Environments}

Note that the prefix \T{env@} only indicates the nature of an environment and is not part of the actual identifier!

\subsection{The Tables}

\begin{environment}{mtabular}{\manArg{Column definition}}
    Sets a \T{tabu}/\T{tabular} table, where the first row automatically becomes the title row and is highlighted accordingly.
\end{environment}

\begin{environment}{mltabular}{\optArg{Header}\manArg{Column definition}}
    Sets a \T{longtabu}/\T{longtable} table, where specifying \T{Header} defines the header column, which is always appended in case of a page break. Should this not be desired, it suffices to not specify the \T{Header} and simply write the first row as the title row as usual,
    which is not placed at the start of every page. The restrictions known and typical for \T{longtable} apply.
\end{environment}

\subsection{General Notes and Tips}

For a \T{longtabu}/\T{longtable} to be displayed correctly, a second compilation pass is usually required.\par{}
It is recommended to use the \env{table} environment together with \cmd{caption} to embed the table sensibly. \env{wrapfigure} is also an option.\par{}
If the \T{sopra-requirements} package is also included, the column types \T{K} and \T{V} can be used for two-column tables.

\subsection{Example}

In the following, two tables are illustrated; note that \envref{mltabular} is automatically centered by its very nature.
\begin{latex}
\begin{mtabular}{lcr}
    Hello & World & \texttt{How}'s it going? \\
    How are & you doing & today then? \\
    Bi & Bu & bo \\
    Hi & Hi & Hi \\
\end{mtabular}
\end{latex}
\textit{Gives:}\par{}
 \begin{mtabular}{lcr}
    Hello & World & \texttt{How}'s it going? \\
    How are & you doing & today then? \\
    Hi & Hi & Hi \\
    Bi & Bu & bo \\
\end{mtabular}\par{}
And similarly:\par{}
\begin{latex}
\begin{mltabular}[I & am & a Header! & Epic-Row]%
        {lcr>{\raggedleft!**!\arraybackslash}m{5cm}}
    Hello & World & \texttt{How}'s it going? & HELLO EARTH \\
    How are & you doing & today then? &  Hi\\
    Bi & Bu & bo & You can tell I'm \\
    Suuper & Long table & because reason  & really creative\\
    A1 & B8 & C1 & D8 \\
    A2 & B7 & C2 & D7 \\
    A3 & B6 & C3 & D6 \\
    A4 & B5 & C4 & D5 \\
    A5 & B4 & C5 & D4 \\
    A6 & B3 & C6 & D3 \\
    A7 & B2 & C7 & D2 \\
    A8 & B1 & C8 & D1 \\
\end{mltabular}
\end{latex}
